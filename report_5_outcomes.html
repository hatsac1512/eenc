Population-level outcomes: the bottom line

Neonatal mortality, skilled birth attendance, postnatal care coverage and breastfeeding rates — the population-level outcomes that signal whether EENC implementation translates into measurably better newborn survival across each country.

Figure 12

Neonatal mortality rates — nine countries, 2008 → 2023

Deaths in the first 28 days of life per 1,000 live births. Every country has improved since 2008. The convergence between low- and high-income countries is one of the most important regional health stories of the period.

✓ China achieved a 73% reduction (10.2 → 2.8) over 15 years — the largest absolute and relative gain. Cambodia, Mongolia, Lao PDR also saw 35-50% reductions. Philippines is an outlier (NMR essentially flat at ~14) reflecting plateaued progress that EENC scale-up may help address.
Table 3

Population coverage of key newborn care indicators

National-level survey data (most recent year per country, 2016–2023) covering eight indicators: skilled birth attendance, facility delivery, caesarean section, skin-to-skin contact, breastfeeding within 1 hour, exclusive breastfeeding (0–28 days), and postnatal care for mothers and newborns within 2 days. Sources: DHS, MICS, national household surveys.

! Facility access is universal in seven of nine countries (skilled birth attendance ≥90%) — but population-level skin-to-skin coverage tells a different story. Vietnam reports only 13% S2S in household surveys, despite EENC implementation in 100% of facilities — suggesting bedside practice has not translated to population-level practice yet.
Figure 13

Caesarean section rate — population level

National caesarean rates (most recent year). WHO recommends 10-15% maximum for medical indication. Three countries are well above; PNG is well below.

! China (40.3%), Viet Nam (34%) and Mongolia (27.1%) exceed WHO's 10-15% threshold significantly. Higher caesarean rates correlate with lower SSC and BF performance regionally. PNG at 3% reflects access constraints, not appropriate restriction.
